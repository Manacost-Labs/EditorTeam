# Edit decision protocol

Use this reference for every rewrite or line edit of an existing draft. It turns editing into a comparative decision: the revision must beat the source at a named reader-facing job, not merely sound different or more polished.

Keep the decision notes internal in clean mode. Surface them only in annotated, review-only, or side-by-side output when they help the user judge a material trade-off.

## 1. Start from keep

Treat each source unit as the current best version until a defect is identified. Work at the smallest unit that contains the problem: phrase, sentence, paragraph, section, or whole piece.

Classify the candidate problem before drafting a replacement:

- **Blocking:** factual drift, broken logic, grammatical failure that changes meaning, unsafe ambiguity, missing condition, or corrupted literal.
- **Material:** the reader must backtrack, cannot find the actor or decision, meets an unsupported claim, loses the relation between ideas, or hears a voice-breaking formula.
- **Optional:** a valid alternative in wording, cadence, punctuation, or order that reflects taste more than reader need.

Fix blocking problems. Fix material problems when the selected intervention permits it. Do not spend the user's edit budget on optional changes unless they asked for alternatives, a new voice, or a deep recast.

## 2. State the change case

Before making a substantive change, be able to answer:

1. What exact problem does the source create for this reader?
2. Which source meaning, fact, qualification, detail, or voice signal must survive?
3. What is the smallest change that removes the problem?
4. How will the candidate be demonstrably better on a cold read?

If the first or fourth answer is only `sounds better`, `flows better`, `more professional`, `cleaner`, or `more engaging`, the diagnosis is incomplete. Name the parse failure, logical gap, repetition, misplaced emphasis, genre mismatch, or voice conflict instead.

## 3. Use the keep-repair-recast ladder

Consider options in this order:

1. **Keep:** the unit already performs its job and contains no in-scope defect.
2. **Repair:** change the smallest span that causes the problem.
3. **Recast:** rebuild the unit only when a local repair leaves the reader problem intact.

Do not recast merely because a fresh sentence is easier to generate than a careful repair. For a difficult unit, draft a minimal repair first. Draft a recast only if needed, then compare both with the source.

The intervention level controls how far the ladder may go:

| Intervention | Default decisions |
|---|---|
| Minimal | Keep everything except blocking errors and strictly local ambiguity |
| Standard | Apply blocking and material repairs; preserve order and strong wording where they work |
| Deep | Recast structure when necessary, but retain protected meaning, evidence, voice anchors, and useful detail |

## 4. Require comparative dominance

A candidate may replace the source only when it:

- resolves the named problem;
- preserves factual, causal, epistemic, and literal invariants;
- retains every piece of unique information that remains in scope;
- creates no new ambiguity, false emphasis, unsupported transition, or genre mismatch;
- does not flatten a useful voice anchor or cadence without a necessary trade-off;
- changes no more text than the chosen intervention requires.

Compare source and candidate across these dimensions:

- fidelity and scope
- logical explicitness
- first-read clarity
- specificity and information density
- voice and cadence
- usefulness for the reader's task
- fit for genre, locale, and destination

The candidate need not be shorter. It may add connective tissue, a named actor, or an explicit relation when the source made the reader infer them. It may be shorter when it removes genuine duplication or unsupported ceremony. Length and detector scores are evidence for review, not the goal.

When a candidate improves one dimension but weakens another, use the writing contract to decide. If the contract does not settle a material trade-off, keep the source or surface the choice instead of silently choosing the editor's taste.

## 5. Audit deletion, compression, and movement

Deletion is justified only when the removed material is:

- a true duplicate whose full meaning survives elsewhere;
- unsupported or misleading and cannot honestly be narrowed;
- outside the agreed scope;
- a wrapper, transition, or performance cue with no unique content;
- an obstruction whose useful content has been relocated intact.

Before deleting a sentence, state its unique payload. If it has one, preserve that payload or record why the contract permits its removal.

After compression, check that examples, conditions, objections, causal steps, and uncertainty did not disappear with the word count. After moving material, re-check pronoun antecedents, chronology, transitions, citation proximity, and paragraph emphasis. A sentence that worked in one location may become misleading in another.

## 6. Protect asymmetry and strong source lines

Good prose often contains deliberate imbalance: one short verdict after a long explanation, an aside, a repeated word with rising force, or an opening that establishes the writer's stance. Do not regularise these features merely because a model can produce a smoother pattern.

Mark source lines that are specific, accurate, memorable, or characteristic. Preserve them unless they conflict with a higher-priority requirement. When one sentence needs repair, do not rewrite its clean neighbours to make the paragraph match the new sentence.

Do not optimise for uniform sentence length, active voice, plain vocabulary, detector counts, paragraph size, or any other single metric. Metrics locate possible trouble; the reader-facing diagnosis decides the edit.

## 7. Adjudicate the diff

After the substantive pass, inspect the changes rather than rereading only the polished result. For every changed unit, assign one internal reason: fidelity, logic, clarity, evidence, voice, genre, consistency, mechanics, or requested adaptation.

Rollback a change when:

- no precise reason can be assigned;
- it merely exchanges one valid expression for another;
- it removes a qualification, example, condition, or authorial judgement;
- it makes the prose more generic, grander, safer, or tidier than the source earned;
- it fixes a metric while worsening meaning, rhythm, or reader orientation;
- a smaller repair can achieve the same result;
- the new sentence needs extra explanation that the source did not need.

Run a cold read on the opening, the densest passage, and the ending. Then compare the revision with the preservation ledger and run applicable deterministic checks.

## Completion test

The edit is complete when every retained change solves a named problem, no protected value has regressed, and the untouched source still contains everything that was already working. Stop when the remaining alternatives are different rather than better.
